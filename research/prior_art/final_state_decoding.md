# Final-state selected-answer decoding is a diagnostic, not a new mechanism

Scoped review, 2026-09-23. [Piwek, Stokes & Summerfield, PLOS Computational Biology 2023](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011555) studied recurrent states under retro-cues and subsequent probes using decoding analyses. [Kozachkov et al., PLOS Computational Biology 2022](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010776) compared working-memory representations and performance under distractors. These papers establish the use of decoders for working-memory states. Their architectures, task labels, biological comparisons and reported effects are not evidence for NeuroTopo's proposed block topology.

In the current synthetic task, each pair shares the same memory and prior prefix but receives a different late selector: `confirm` retains the prior candidate; `revise` selects one of three alternatives. A final-state decoder trained and evaluated specifically on the **revise** rows asks if the selected answer is linearly accessible after late evidence. The pair, rather than its two correlated rows, is the unit for train/validation/test splitting; training may use both rows, while primary evaluation uses one revise row per pair.

This is observational linear decoding of an already trained recurrent state. A newly fitted answer head has extra labelled examples and a different objective from the original shared readout. High accuracy does not show that the original model head used the information. Low accuracy cannot establish that the information was destroyed: nonlinear cue×value binding, training-sample size, regularization and the chosen decoder family remain alternatives. No semantic trajectory specialization, adaptive mechanism, biological advantage or Gate1 advancement follows from this diagnostic alone.
