# EXP-0008 v1 — Final revise-answer linear access after late evidence

Registered 2026-09-23 before implementation and EXP-0008 outcomes. EXP-0006 found near-ceiling linear access to candidate labels **before** late evidence, and EXP-0007 found directional loss when a short-delay linear mapping was applied to longer-delay states. This new diagnostic asks about the state **after** the late selector. No recurrent architecture, graph, pretrained checkpoint or training schedule changes; Gate1 remains closed. Prior art: `research/prior_art/final_state_decoding.md` and the existing RIMs/TRNN/conn2res matrix. Measured link: frozen dynamics → final-state representation → fixed linear answer readout.

## Question and strongest rejection

At delay32, is the **late-selected label on revise trials** at least3 percentage points less linearly accessible in the independent-block model's final hidden state than in the degree/self-loop-matched random-mask model? A confirm trial can reuse the prior answer, while revise chooses one of the three alternatives. The task's final label itself is not present in the selector token.

The probe is trained with new labelled delay32 examples and a different objective from the pretrained shared task head. High accessibility does not mean the original model used the information; low linear accessibility can reflect nonlinear cue×candidate binding, probe capacity, state geometry, regularization or finite data rather than destruction of information by the late-evidence update. No causal mediation, semantic trajectory or biological conclusion follows.

## Primary metric, guards and decision

Primary: mean **revise-only** final-label accuracy of A probe on the fresh delay32 test pairs. For each trained model seed, compute `d_seed=M3−C0`; summarize all ten differences, sample SD, paired-seed 20,000-resample percentile bootstrap95% CI with RNG seed `1040001`. New test pairs are shared across models, but they are not 2,048 independent trained-model replications. B heads are only a refitting-stability check; they are never selected if they score higher.

Prerequisites: all ten seeds and three model records complete; checkpoint original-score replay, source/data/label/selector hashes, pair split, model/state/head resource parity, train-only scaling and readout equality all pass. Positive-control adequacy: C0 and contextual wide model have mean **validation revise** accuracy≥0.90 and at least eight of ten seeds≥0.85; M3 has no imposed floor. For M3/C0 the mean absolute difference between A and B **test revise** accuracy must be≤0.02, or probe refitting is too unstable. Behavioral guard on the same new final pairs: the original model's **revise final-answer** accuracy in M3 must trail C0 by at least1 point in mean, and both must exceed0.50. If any prerequisite fails, INCONCLUSIVE; no refit or endpoint change.

**SUPPORTED** for a ≥3pp linear-access deficit requires the paired 95% CI upper<−0.03. **PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED** if CI upper<0 but the 3pp magnitude is not confirmed. **FALSIFIED** for that ≥3pp deficit if CI lower>−0.03 and the partial rule did not apply. Otherwise INCONCLUSIVE. Evaluate in this order. The prior EXP-0004 result and Gate1 status are immutable.

Secondary only: A/B prior and revise validation/test scores, confirm final-label probe accuracy, all-pair final success, original pretrained pre/confirm/revise/joint scores on the same test split, delay64 results, and probe-versus-original-score gaps. Any positive secondary result is descriptive and cannot replace the single revise delay32 endpoint. A near-ceiling decoder limits sensitivity to smaller representation differences.
